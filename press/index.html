Bible Buddy Press

Bible Buddy is a reading plan that tells you what to read today, and gets you back on track when life happens.

Bible Buddy is a Bible reading plan app for iPhone and iPad. It keeps the plan and the progress: today's passages with a check per reading, the streak, and a buffer for busy days. It includes no Bible text, so it works with the Bible the reader already has, printed or in any app. Behind by a week? Catch up a few days per sitting and see the date you are caught up, or move the plan so today is the next unread day; nothing is skipped. There is no account, no advertising and no analytics; plans and progress stay on the reader's devices and in their own iCloud. The app is free with the Bible in a Year plan and five more; Bible Buddy Pro (specialized plans, the plan builder with its on-device assistant, plan import and sharing) is $0.99 a month or $9.99 a year with a 14-day trial.

What sets it apart

  • Plans, not text: the reader keeps the Bible they already read, and the app keeps track.
  • "Behind is not broken": catch up in sittings of a few days, one book at a time, or move the plan to today.
  • Read ahead: tomorrow's passages today, and Today shows how many days ahead you are.
  • Start a plan on any date, on the weekdays you choose, and from the day you are on if you are already mid-plan.
  • A streak that counts reading days, so a rest day in the schedule does not break it.
  • Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets with a tap per reading; one daily reminder at the hour you choose.
  • Pro: chronological, M'Cheyne style, the Bible in 90 days, two tracks with grace days, and a plan builder whose assistant drafts a plan from a sentence, on the device.
  • No account, no ads, no analytics, no servers; iCloud sync through the reader's own private database.
  • English, Dutch, German, French, Italian, Spanish and Brazilian Portuguese; VoiceOver and Dynamic Type throughout.

About the developer

Raphael Koebraam is a software engineer who builds Bible Buddy alone. He made it for the reading plan he had kept in a spreadsheet: to know what to read today without opening a table, and to get back on track after a missed week without skipping anything.
